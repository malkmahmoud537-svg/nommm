Oceanographic Overview

THE WORLD BENEATH THE WAVES

Vast, deep, and essential to all terrestrial life, our global ocean is a single, interconnected liquid biosphere holding 97% of Earth's water.

The Planetary Cradle
3.8 Billion Years of Continuous Marine Evolution

What is the Ocean?

The ocean is a continuous body of saltwater covering 361 million square kilometers (71%) of the planet. It comprises over 1.33 billion cubic kilometers of water with an average salinity of 35 parts per thousand, forming Earth’s largest thermal heat reservoir.

Why are Oceans Important?

Every second breath we take comes from the ocean. Marine phytoplankton (prochlorococcus and diatoms) produce 50% to 80% of the world's atmospheric oxygen. The ocean also provides food security and livelihood for over 3 billion people worldwide.

How Oceans Regulate Climate

The oceans absorb 90% of excess anthropogenic heat trapped by greenhouse gases and capture approximately 30% of global carbon emissions. The Global Ocean Conveyor Belt (thermohaline circulation) circulates warm water from the equator to poles, moderating continental weather.

What Lives Beneath?

Scientists estimate between 700,000 and 1 million eukaryotic marine species inhabit our oceans, yet less than 25% have been formally described. Life spans from microscopic zooplankton to the colossal 190-ton blue whale.

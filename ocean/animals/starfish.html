Ochre Sea Star

Pisaster ochraceus • Purple Starfish

Scientific Classification

Kingdom:Animalia
Phylum:Echinodermata
Class:Asteroidea
Order:Forcipulatida
Family:Asteriidae
Genus:Pisaster

The foundational archetype of the "keystone species" ecological principle, capable of regenerating lost limbs and using hydraulic suction feet to pry open mussels.

Habitat
Rocky Intertidal
Diet
Mussels, Barnacles, Snails
Lifespan
Up to 20 Years
Radius
15 – 28 cm

Water Vascular System & External Digestion

Rather than relying on blood, sea stars utilize a water vascular system that uses pressurized seawater to operate thousands of tiny hydraulic suction tube feet. To feed on bivalve California mussels, the sea star pulls the shells apart just 0.1 mm, then everts its cardiac stomach inside out through its mouth into the mussel shell, releasing powerful digestive enzymes to liquefy the prey inside its own shell.

The Keystone Species Ecological Principle

In a historic 1966 experiment by ecologist Robert Paine, removing ochre stars from tidal rocks caused California mussels to completely monopolize the shoreline within months, driving out 25 species of algae, sponges, and limpets. This proved that a single keystone predator preserves ecosystem biodiversity.

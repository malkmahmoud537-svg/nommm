Green Sea Turtle

Chelonia mydas • Pacific & Atlantic Green Turtle

Scientific Classification

Kingdom:Animalia
Phylum:Chordata
Class:Reptilia
Order:Testudines
Family:Cheloniidae
Genus:Chelonia

Ancient marine wanderers that have plied Earth’s oceans for over 110 million years, outliving the dinosaurs and maintaining the health of coastal seagrass meadows.

Habitat
Tropical Reefs & Bays
Adult Diet
Herbivore (Seagrass)
Average Lifespan
70 – 80+ Years
Adult Weight
130 – 200 kg

Physical Characteristics & Green Fat

Contrary to popular belief, their name does not come from their outer shell (carapace), which is typically olive-brown or mottled amber, but from the distinct emerald green color of their subdermal fat tissue. This pigmentation is caused by their strictly herbivorous adult diet of seagrass and marine macro-algae. Their streamlined heart-shaped shell and paddle-shaped flippers allow them to swim gracefully for thousands of miles.

Natal Homing & Ocean Navigation

One of nature's greatest navigational feats: mature females migrate thousands of nautical miles across open oceans to return to the precise beach where they hatched decades earlier. They accomplish this by reading the geomagnetic signatures of Earth's magnetic field (geomagnetic imprinting). Females crawl ashore under cover of darkness to dig a deep flask-shaped nest and deposit 100 to 120 spherical eggs.

Fascinating Biological Facts

  • Temperature-Dependent Sex Determination: The temperature of the sand determines the sex of the hatchlings ("Cool dudes, hot babes"). Sand temperatures above 29.1°C produce females, while cooler sands produce males.
  • Marathon Freedivers: While sleeping underwater wedged beneath coral ledges, sea turtles can slow their heart rate to one beat every several minutes and hold their breath for up to 5 hours.
  • Seagrass Gardeners: By grazing on the tips of seagrass blades, turtles stimulate new shoots, maintaining the productive nurseries essential for shrimp, lobsters, and reef fish.
Severe Modern Threats & Plastic Ingestion

Over 52% of all sea turtles worldwide have ingested synthetic plastic waste, mistaking buoyant plastic shopping bags for jellyfish. Rising sand temperatures driven by global warming are causing catastrophic feminization of rookeries (in Australia's northern Great Barrier Reef, over 99% of new green turtle hatchlings are now female). Entanglement in commercial shrimp trawl nets and coastal artificial light pollution disorienting newly emerged hatchlings exacerbate their extinction risk.

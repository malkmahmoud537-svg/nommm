Great White Shark

Carcharodon carcharias • White Pointer

Scientific Classification

Kingdom:Animalia

The ocean's preeminent macro-predator, Great White Sharks are keystone architects of oceanic trophic balance, regulating pinniped and ray populations.

Physical Characteristics & Senses

Great Whites feature robust torpedo-shaped bodies with dark slate-gray dorsal coloring and crisp white ventral countershading, rendering them virtually invisible when viewed from above against dark depths or from below against bright sunlit shallows. Their snouts contain hundreds of electro-receptive pores called the Ampullae of Lorenzini, capable of detecting billionths of a volt emitted by the muscle contractions of prey.

Predatory Biology & Endothermy

Unlike most ectothermic fish, great whites possess a specialized vascular counter-current heat exchanger system called the rete mirabile. This allows them to maintain internal body and stomach temperatures up to 14°C warmer than the surrounding cold sea, granting explosive bursts of speed (up to 56 km/h) to launch their entire 2,000 kg body airborne ("breaching") when ambushing cape fur seals.

Fascinating Biological Facts

  • Revolving Serrated Teeth: Up to 300 triangular serrated teeth arranged in multiple rows. A single shark will cycle through more than 20,000 teeth in a lifetime.
  • The White Shark Café: Satellite tracking tags reveal individuals migrate thousands of miles into an empty oceanic region between Hawaii and California to socialize and forage.
  • Slow Maturity: Males take 26 years and females up to 33 years to reach sexual maturity, making populations exceptionally fragile to overexploitation.
Conservation Status & Human Threats

Unregulated shark finning, accidental commercial longline bycatch, protective coastal beach gill nets ("shark culling"), and trophy hunting have caused global populations to plummet. As apex predators, their decline leads to mesopredator release, collapsing seagrass and shellfish populations.

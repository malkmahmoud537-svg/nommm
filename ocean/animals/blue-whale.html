Blue Whale

Balaenoptera musculus • Rorqual Baleen Whale

Scientific Classification

Kingdom:Animalia
Phylum:Chordata
Class:Mammalia
Order:Artiodactyla
Family:Balaenopteridae
Genus:Balaenoptera

The blue whale is the largest known creature to ever inhabit planet Earth, eclipsing even the largest titanosaur dinosaurs in mass.

Habitat
Pelagic Oceans
Diet
Euphausiids (Krill)
Average Lifespan
80 – 90 Years
Maximum Weight
190+ Metric Tons

Physical Characteristics & Anatomy

With a long, streamlined mottled blue-gray body, the blue whale's heart alone weighs approximately 400 pounds (180 kg)—the size of a golf cart. Its tongue weighs as much as an entire adult elephant. Rather than teeth, its upper jaw contains up to 400 overlapping baleen plates made of keratin that strain enormous volumes of seawater.

Dietary Habits & Lunge-Feeding

Despite their colossal size, blue whales feed almost exclusively on tiny euphausiid crustaceans known as krill. An adult consumes up to 4 metric tons (40 million krill) daily during high-latitude polar feeding seasons. They perform energetic "lunge-feeding"—accelerating into dense krill swarms, expanding their ventral pleated grooves to engulf up to 100 metric tons of water in a single mouthful, and expelling water through their baleen.

Fascinating Biological Facts

  • The Loudest Animal on Earth: Blue whale low-frequency infrasonic pulses reach 188 decibels and travel across hundreds of nautical miles through deep underwater sound channels (SOFAR channel).
  • Calf Growth Rate: A newborn calf weighs approximately 3 tons and drinks 400 liters of mother’s milk daily, gaining up to 90 kg (200 lbs) every 24 hours.
  • Planetary Carbon Pump: Over its lifetime, a single blue whale sequesters an average of 33 tons of carbon dioxide, sinking it permanently to the ocean floor upon death.
Conservation Status & Modern Threats

20th-century industrial whaling decimated global blue whale populations by over 99%, driving numbers from 350,000 to fewer than 2,000 individuals. Although legally protected today, blue whales face lethal collisions with container ships, entanglements in commercial fishing lines, anthropogenic acoustic noise pollution disrupting communication, and climate-induced declines in polar krill biomass.
